# Benchmark against Orekit

Generated by `tools/bench_orekit.py` (rerun it to refresh) with Orekit via orekit-jpype 13.1.9.0, Java 21.0.12.1. Orekit gets the same data as the simulator (leap seconds, zero EOP, J2-J4, the CelesTrak space-weather file, the same Sun and Moon models), so differences come from the implementations. Orekit runs DormandPrince853 at 1e-14 relative tolerance and is the reference. Differences are the largest over the run, at the output times, in metres: radial / in-track / cross-track / total.

## Reading the results

- **Gravity and the integrator.** With rtol 1e-12, DOP853 stays within a few millimetres of Orekit over 1 to 30 days, from LEO to Molniya to GEO. At rtol 1e-10 (the default) it is within centimetres to half a metre, and RK4 at 10 s within about 0.5 to 3 m, growing along-track. These are the simulator's own integration errors: Orekit is converged far below them.
- **Drag.** Densities agree with Orekit's NRLMSISE-00 to about 1e-5, using the same space-weather file through Orekit's own reader. On a quiet day the trajectories agree to millimetres. On the May 2024 storm day the difference is about 0.2 m after a day, out of several kilometres of drag decay: the simulator stops its steps at each 3-hour space-weather change, which Orekit's integrator does not do, and F10.7 handling between days differs slightly.
- **Sun and Moon.** With the same ephemerides, the third-body force matches Orekit's to about 1 cm over 30 days in GEO.
- **SGP4.** Identical to Orekit's SGP4/SDP4 in TEME (micrometres). The few-metre GCRF difference is the TEME-to-GCRF rotation, which the simulator approximates with IAU 2006/2000B precession-nutation and the equation of the equinoxes.
- **Not compared.** SRP (Orekit uses a conical shadow with penumbra, the simulator a cylindrical shadow), the accuracy of the analytic Sun and Moon positions themselves, and real Earth-orientation data, all of which are the same by construction here or differ by design.
